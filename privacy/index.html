Privacy Policy

Last updated 6 October 2026 · App: com.railbuzz.app · Beta release

RailBuzz shows live Indian Railways information and lets passengers on the same train talk to each other. This policy describes exactly what the app collects, why, and who it is shared with. It covers the RailBuzz Android app and nothing else.

How long chat is kept

Who can see itHow long
Passengers in the roomUntil 15 minutes after the train completes its run
RailBuzz, for safety review3 days, then deleted

Messages other passengers reacted to, reported or blocked over, with the three before and after them14 days, our moderators only

A train’s chat room exists for that journey only. Once the train arrives the room closes for passengers, and its messages and help requests are deleted within three days.

Where a message has been reported, we keep a copy of it and of the surrounding conversation for up to 90 days, so the report can be reviewed properly and so we can respond to a lawful request from the police or a government authority.

Where other passengers liked, disliked, reported or blocked someone over a message, we keep a copy of that message and of the three messages before and after it for 14 days, so our moderators can see what a contribution score rests on. These two kinds of copy are the only chat that outlives the three days, and both are deleted automatically when their period ends.

Deleting your account does not shorten the three days. Your messages disappear from every room immediately and nobody using RailBuzz can see them, but a copy only our safety team can reach is held for the same three days that already apply to everyone else, and then erased. Otherwise deleting an account would be the quickest way to destroy the evidence of harassing somebody. The same number also cannot register again during those three days. Your help requests, likes and dislikes, the trains you follow, your live chat phone checks and your contribution score are deleted at once. Full detail on the deletion page.

The live chat phone check

Live chat opens once your mobile number has been verified on the phone you are using, with the code from the SMS: when you sign in with your number, when you verify it in your profile, or when Join Chat asks. When the code arrives, Android asks whether RailBuzz may read that one message; if you tap Allow, the code is filled in from it. RailBuzz has no permission to read your SMS and never sees any other message. A code you type yourself still signs you in or verifies the number, but live chat then asks you to verify it from the SMS.

We record when the number was verified and how, with a scrambled form of the number and a random ID that the app makes for itself and sends with its requests to our server, so we know which phone verified it. Live chat then stays open on that phone for 180 days, also after you sign out. It asks again after that, when the SIM is changed or removed, when your number changes, and on a new phone or after reinstalling the app.

To notice a SIM being changed or removed, the app keeps a scrambled summary of which SIM slots hold a SIM and, when there is only one, which network it is on. It needs no permission for this, and the summary never leaves your phone.

Changing your mobile number

You can move your account to a new number from your profile or Settings. Your Google account must be linked to RailBuzz first — if you signed up with your number, the app asks you to link it: your Gmail stays with your account, so you can always sign back in, even without your old SIM. The new number is then verified with the code from the SMS on this phone, as for live chat; a code you type yourself doesn’t count here. Live chat stays open on this phone with the new number. Your alias, reputation and followed trains stay with your account. You can change your number once every 30 days.

We record each change: when it happened, which phone made it (the random ID above), and the old and new numbers in scrambled form. The record is kept for a year, also if you delete your account in the meantime. For three days the old number can’t start or join another RailBuzz account, and a chat block that is running carries on with your new number and stays with the old one until it ends. We email your Google address to tell you the number changed, with only the last four digits of the new one.

Location, specifically

We do not store your location, and we never send your coordinates to our servers.

Your position is used on your device to work out three things: the nearest railway station, how far you are from the track of the train whose chat room you have opened, and which town and state you are in. Only results leave the device: the distance as a coarse range such as “2–10 km”, and, when you ask the assistant for railway news, the town and state, such as “Secunderabad, Telangana”, so it can choose news for your area. Never a latitude and longitude.

Turning a position into a town name is done by Android’s built-in geocoder, which on most phones asks Google’s location service; RailBuzz does not receive the coordinates. There is no location history, and where you are is never shown to other passengers.

Location is optional. If you decline the permission, everything else in the app continues to work; you will just need to pick your station yourself.

What other passengers can see

Only your passenger alias, the messages and help requests you post, and how many likes and dislikes each message has. Your mobile number, name, email, location and contribution score are never shown to anyone else using the app, and nobody can see which messages you liked or disliked.

Chat rooms are public to the passengers in them. Please do not post anything in a room that you would not want those people to read.

Automated safety checks on chat rooms

Chat rooms are monitored automatically for signs that a conversation has turned abusive — a spike in reports and blocks, several people turning on one person, or language associated with threats, sexual harassment, hate, scams or someone’s private details being posted.

Most of this happens inside our own database and nothing leaves it. When those signals suggest a room may have turned abusive, a recent extract of that room’s messages is sent to Google Gemini for a second opinion, because words alone cannot tell an argument from a threat. Before it is sent:

  • passenger names are replaced with placeholders (P1, P2, and so on)
  • phone numbers and email addresses are removed
  • no account identifier, train number or travel date is included

The result is only ever shown to our moderators. It never automatically removes a message, restricts an account or closes a room — a person decides that. We deliberately do not let an automated system silence a chat room, because a room may be the fastest way passengers have to warn each other about a real emergency.

Contribution scores

Each night we work out a contribution score for passengers who chatted, from what other passengers did: how many different people liked or disliked their messages, replied to them or found a reply helpful, and any reports, blocks or posting blocks. How much someone writes earns nothing. Praise counts for less as it ages, and so, more slowly, does misconduct.

The score is seen only by our moderators. We use it to find the passengers who help others most — in future, for badges and a Trusted Contributor status — and those who cause problems. It never restricts an account by itself; a person decides any action.

To check whether a reaction was fair, a message other passengers liked, disliked, reported or blocked over may be sent to OpenAI with the three messages before and after it. Before it is sent:

  • passenger names are replaced with placeholders (P1, P2, and so on)
  • phone numbers and email addresses are removed
  • no account identifier, train number or travel date is included

OpenAI answers only whether each reaction fits what was said. It never gives a score: our own fixed rules turn its answers into points, and a moderator’s decision on a report always overrides it.

Your choices

  • Delete your account and data at any time, from the app menu. See Deleting your RailBuzz account.
  • Turn off location in Android settings. The app keeps working.
  • Turn off notifications in Android settings, or mute a chat room from its menu.
  • Stop following a train at any time.
  • Take back a like or dislike by tapping it again.
  • Reshuffle your alias from the profile screen.
  • Ask for a copy of your data by emailing us.

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act you also have the right to correct or complete inaccurate data, to have it erased, to withdraw your consent at any time, to grievance redressal, and to nominate another person to exercise these rights on your behalf if you die or become incapacitated. To exercise any of them, write to the Grievance Officer named below.

Security

Traffic between the app and our servers uses HTTPS. Access to your data is enforced at the database level, so one passenger cannot read another's profile, private details or reports. This is a beta release, and while we take security seriously, no system can be guaranteed perfectly secure.

Children

RailBuzz is for adults. You must be 18 or older to create an account, and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone younger. The app puts you in conversation with strangers, and we do not operate the parental-consent mechanisms that Indian law requires for users under 18. If you believe a minor has created an account, email us and we will close it.

How long we keep an inactive account

If you do not use RailBuzz for three years, we will delete your account and personal data. We will email or notify you at least 48 hours beforehand, so you can open the app and keep it if you want to.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, we will update the date at the top and notify you in the app.

Contact, and your right to complain

Our Grievance Officer, who is also our contact person for personal data, is Kiran Kumar — grievance@railbuzz.in.

If you are not satisfied, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India about a data matter, or appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee (gac.gov.in) about a content decision. Full detail, including what to include in a complaint, is on Grievance Redressal.
